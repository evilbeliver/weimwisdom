# The Badge of Honor: Navigating the "Lumpy Bumpy" Years

## A Love-Hate Relationship

Many dog owners have never heard of fatty tissue deposits, better known as [Lipomas](https://www.petmd.com/dog/condition/skin/lipoma-in-dogs), until they find one during a belly rub. But for those of us who have shared our lives with Weimaraners, these "lumps and bumps" are practically a rite of passage. Certain breeds seem more susceptible to them, and our beloved "Gray Ghosts" happen to be right at the top of that list.

While we are used to seeing these and understand what they typically are, we make it a point to have every single lump checked by our vet. This is especially true for Loki, who has already bravely battled cancer once. We never ignore a new discovery; we've simply come to terms with the reality of "Weim Life."

**I can't stress this enough: We take every new lump seriously until it is validated by a professional.**

Weims seem to hit this "lumpy bumpy" phase right as they cross into their mature years, typically between 7 and 10 years of age. You know the phase—they still act like puppies at heart, but the gray starts creeping into their muzzles (and no, I don't mean that beautiful Weimaraner silver!).

## The Traveling Lipoma

Loki currently has a large one on his leg and a few on his chest. If you've ever seen the movie Austin Powers in Goldmember, you'll remember the hilarious "Mole! Mole! Mole!" scenes where the mole seems to move every time the camera cuts. That is Loki's lipoma on his leg! It seems to travel a bit here and there, but since it isn't on a joint and doesn't bother him, we just take it in stride.

Why do I call it a double-edged sword? Well, for Loki, he wasn't supposed to be with us this long. Every day is a gift. So, while seeing him age is a reminder of our limited time, these bumps have become a badge of honor. In our early days as dog parents, we might have dreaded them, but now we celebrate them. They are physical proof of a life well-lived and a journey shared.

## Looking Forward

We have a while before Paige hits this stage, but for now, we will embrace every new lump on Loki as a symbol of his resilience (while still keeping our vet on speed dial!). This boy has earned every bit of his "Golden Years."
